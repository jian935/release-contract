# Changelog

Numbers here are measurements from this repository, not marketing. Anything
not measured is marked as such.

## 0.3.0 — 2026-11-09

**Every disagreement across three blind rounds now has a rule attached to it,
and two silent release paths are closed.**

- Published blind round 3 under `eval/blind/rounds/round3/`: agreement **0.7910**,
  Wilson 95% [0.6793, 0.8712] over the same 67 segments. This reader used the
  point estimate (like round 1) and took the same side as round 2 on both rule
  questions, so its 14 disagreements are exactly 10 Wilson-class + 4 rule-class.
- With three readers, **every one of the 37 disagreements across all rounds now
  falls into one of two rule questions, with zero unexplained**. The three rates
  are exactly `1 - 10/67`, `1 - 4/67`, `1 - 14/67`; pairwise kappa is 0.6554 /
  0.8929 / 0.7498. Documented as the primary result in `eval/blind/README.md`
  and each round record.
- Both open rule questions are now **2 of 3 readers against the engine**, reached
  independently from different statistical readings. Still not patched: the cost
  and the consequence are recorded in `DESIGN.md`, and changing precedence would
  re-score every published round.
- `eval/blind/next_round/README.md` now says the same-case well is nearly dry: a
  fourth reader on these 67 segments adds a kappa point, while the useful next
  round is **new cases nobody has seen**.
- Added `scripts/whatif_rules.py`: prices the two open rule questions from the
  blind rounds in verdicts rather than opinions. Lowering effective harm one rank
  for a configured fallback would move 3 of the 67 published segments from
  `FENCE` to `AUTO`; letting a hard violation outrank `G1` would move 2 from
  `HOLD` to `FENCE`. Neither is implemented, and `tests/test_whatif_rules.py`
  holds each scenario to its direction.
- **Fixed a silent release path:** a `NaN` cost used to pass every comparison
  (`NaN >= 0` is False, `NaN > limit` is also False) and the segment came back
  `AUTO`. Non-finite numbers are now refused at parse time and in the loader,
  and `nan` / `inf` in a counts CSV is rejected with the row number.
- Numeric fields are type-checked before arithmetic: `"n": "200"` used to
  surface as `'<' not supported between instances of 'str' and 'int'`, and
  `"n": true` slipped through because `isinstance(True, int)` is True.
- A UTF-8 BOM in front of a contract is now tolerated instead of failing with
  "Unexpected UTF-8 BOM" — Excel and Notepad add one.
- `decide --json` now echoes the effective `guardrails`, so a decision records
  the thresholds it was made under even when the block was omitted.
- New advisory risk flag `success_floor_is_zero`: a `min_success_rate` of 0 makes
  gate G2 vacuous, so the decision says so instead of looking like a pass.
- Added `tests/test_bad_input.py` (19 cases) fixing what must be rejected, what
  is accepted on purpose, and that every bad contract exits 3 without a
  traceback.
- Added `scripts/check_rollback_baseline.py` and `release_contract/launch.py`:
  the post-launch half of the trigger promise. It replays a launch log against
  the baselines `decide` recorded, window by window. A partial window never
  fires, a healthy window resets the run, a segment missing from the log is
  reported as `no observations`, and non-`AUTO` segments get no baseline claim.
  `--fail-on-trigger` exits 2 for a pipeline.
- `ROLLBACK_CONSECUTIVE_WINDOWS` is now a single constant in the engine, used by
  the trigger text and by the checker, so the two cannot disagree.
- Added `examples/launch/`: a **SYNTHETIC** log (generated by
  `scripts/gen_launch_example.py`) where an `AUTO` segment clears its baseline
  for two windows and then misses it twice. A self-check requires the file to
  match its generator, stay labelled synthetic, and still fire a trigger — an
  example that silently stops illustrating the point is worse than none.
- Published blind round 2 under `eval/blind/rounds/round2/`: sheet, frozen
  result, and a record of the four disagreements. Agreement **0.9403**, Wilson
  95% [0.8563, 0.9765] over the same 67 segments, from a second independent
  reader. Reader-vs-reader agreement is 0.791 raw / Cohen's kappa 0.6554, so the
  two rates are now always quoted together.
- Round 2's reader applied a Wilson lower bound unprompted; 12 rationales quote
  it and all 12 match `wilson_lower` within 0.05pp. G2 is therefore no longer the
  contested gate — the remaining four disagreements are rule precedence
  (fallback vs harm ceiling; `HOLD` vs `FENCE` when evidence is thin *and* a hard
  ceiling is violated), written up as open questions in `DESIGN.md`.
- The published-round self-check now requires **every** round's rate to be
  quoted in all three public docs, not just the newest one, so a better number
  cannot bury a worse one.
- Added `assets/demo-recording.gif`: a real screen capture of one run (26 s,
  800px, console window only) with `assets/demo-recording.json` holding its
  SHA-256, the host and exactly what was scaled. A new self-check fails when the
  sidecar and the file disagree, because a capture cannot be regenerated.
- `scripts/demo.py` now invokes `sys.executable` instead of `python`. A Windows
  launcher stub could shadow `python`, exit 0 and print nothing, which produced
  a recording of four commands with no output. It also prints a warning when a
  step succeeds while printing nothing, so that failure can no longer be silent.
- The last demo step reports its real exit code instead of printing the report a
  second time; the duplicated report pushed the verdict table out of view.
- The README animation is `assets/demo.gif`, rendered from real stdout by
  `scripts/render_demo_gif.py`. A SHA-256 sidecar lets the self-check fail when
  the engine changes without re-rendering — it is a rendering, not a screen
  capture, and it says so in both the README and the sidecar.

- Added `eval/blind/next_batch/`: **30 new cases / 38 segments** that nobody has
  annotated, generated by `scripts/gen_next_batch.py`. Three readers have used
  up the first 67 segments, so a fourth round on those buys one kappa point
  instead of a new rule question. The batch targets inputs the first one never
  tested — four-segment contracts, `n = 0`, `degrade`/`rollback` carrying a
  non-zero review cost, and ceilings other than `medium` — and three cases are
  deliberate pairs that differ in exactly one field. A model cross-check was run
  on it (below).
- `blind_eval.py` and `publish_round.py` now take `--prompts`, so a second batch
  is scored against its own prompt file. Without the flag they read the first
  batch and **refuse** every row of a new sheet, rather than silently scoring a
  smaller denominator. Two tests hold that: the batch sheet scores 1/1 against
  its own prompts and raises against the old ones.
- Three project locks added (24 now): the batch prompts must stay answer-free,
  share no case id or segment name with the first batch, and match the
  generator byte for byte; the batch sheet must stay blank, reproducible, and
  use the same 15 columns as the first sheet; and the model cross-check must
  stay labelled `kind: model_check` with an annotator that says "model", so it
  can never be cited as a human round.
- Ran a **model cross-check** on that batch and kept it out of `rounds/`: a
  language model answered all 38 segments at **0.8947** (34/38), Wilson 95%
  [0.7587, 0.9583]. It is not a fourth reader. Its four disagreements are still
  one real question — G4's floor (`medium` or `high`) and whether
  `degrade`/`rollback` count as a fallback — recorded as open question 3 in
  `DESIGN.md` and priced at 0/1 and 1/3 flipped segments. **Not implemented:**
  adopting both would make that sheet 38/38, which is an agreement produced by
  editing the rule until it agrees, not a measurement.
- `publish_round.py` now records `kind` (default `blind_round`) and the `prompts`
  path in `result.json`, and both the self-check and `quick_validate.py` read
  them. A model's answers therefore cannot be published as a blind round, and a
  round on another case set is recomputed against its own questions instead of
  failing with every row reported as an unknown case id.

## 0.2.0 — 2026-11-09

**Usable as a skill, not just a script.**

- Added `init`: writes a structurally valid starter contract whose `n=0`
  makes it decide `HOLD`. A fresh user can reach a first decision without
  reading the schema by hand.
- Added `validate`: checks structure, enum values and coverage without
  deciding, with `--json` for tooling.
- Added `import`: builds a contract from a CSV of raw counts (`id`, `n`,
  `successes`, optional segment fields). A `success_rate` / `accuracy` column
  is refused with the reason, and unknown columns are errors, so a typo cannot
  become a silent default. `examples/counts.example.csv` shows the shape.
- Added `--version` and `version`; `diff --fail-on-regression` returns exit 2
  when any segment got worse.
- Exit codes are now a documented interface: `0` ok, `2` opted-in gate hit,
  `3` unusable input. Invalid input prints one line on stderr instead of a
  traceback.
- `pyproject.toml` added: `pip install .` provides a `release-contract`
  console script. Version must now agree across `__init__.py`, both READMEs
  and `pyproject.toml`; the self-check fails otherwise.
- `SKILL.md` rewritten as an agent runbook: four-step workflow, field table
  with ranges and enums, exit-code table, troubleshooting table.
- Added `DESIGN.md`: each decision paired with the alternative it beat and the
  evidence that would reopen it.
- Property scan grew from five to eight invariants: segment-order invariance,
  "more successes never make a verdict stricter", and "raising
  `min_success_rate` never loosens a verdict". The scanner now accepts an
  injected decider so tests can plant a bug and prove the checks fire.
- Blind round 1 published under `eval/blind/rounds/round1/`: sheet, frozen
  result and a self-check that recomputes the published rate from the sheet
  and fails on drift. Agreement 0.8507, Wilson 95% [0.7466, 0.9169] over 67
  segments; all ten disagreements are the Wilson gate and none runs in the
  dangerous direction.
- Added `scripts/compare_rounds.py`: inter-annotator agreement (raw + Cohen's
  kappa) on the segments two rounds share, so a second reader can tell
  "conservative tool" apart from "one reader's habit".
- Added `scripts/logs_to_counts.py`: runtime JSONL (`segment`, `ok`) to the
  counts CSV that `import` consumes. Records without an outcome are excluded
  from `n` and reported, never counted as failures.
- Added `scripts/demo.py`: paced replay of the flow for screen recording.

## 0.1.0 — 2026-11-08

Initial local build.

- Six deterministic gates, Wilson lower bound instead of the point estimate,
  four traffic buckets that always sum to 1.0.
- `decide` / `diff` / `gates` / `eval` / `version`, `--fail-on`, `--json`.
- Self-built dev/hold-out suites: 25 cases each, 32 and 35 scored segments,
  verdict accuracy 1.0000 with Wilson 95% [0.8928, 1.0] and [0.9011, 1.0].
  These are rule-consistency numbers from author-written labels, not
  effectiveness evidence.
- Property scan over a fixed seed, blind protocol, answer-free packets and a
  release self-check suite.
