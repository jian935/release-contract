# Blind evaluation protocol

**Four human rounds are published, each with the sheet it was scored from, plus
one model cross-check kept deliberately separate.**

| round | case set | agreement | Wilson 95% | disagreements |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | first, 67 segs | **0.8507** | [0.7466, 0.9169] | 10, all Wilson-class |
| 2 | first, 67 segs | **0.9403** | [0.8563, 0.9765] | 4, all rule-class |
| 3 | first, 67 segs | **0.7910** | [0.6793, 0.8712] | 14 = 10 Wilson + 4 rule |
| 4 | **second, 38 segs** | **0.8158** | [0.6658, 0.9078] | 7 = 2 Wilson + 5 rule |

Every rate is recomputed from `rounds/roundN/answers.csv` by
`scripts/publish_round.py` and re-verified by the project self-check. A language
model also answered the 38 segments of round 4 and is archived in `model_check/`
at **0.8947**; it is not a round and not a reader.

**Rounds 1–3 are one engine against three human readers on the same 67 fabricated
segments; round 4 is a different 38-segment set.** Across all 37 disagreements in
rounds 1–3, every one falls into one of two rule questions and **none is
unexplained**. The rates are exactly `1 - 10/67`, `1 - 4/67` and `1 - 14/67`:

- **Wilson-class:** observed rate clears the floor, lower bound does not, no other
  gate violated — 10 fixed cases. A reader who reasons on the point estimate
  produces all 10; a reader who uses the lower bound produces none.
- **Rule-class:** 4 fixed cases, the two open questions in `DESIGN.md` (does a
  human fallback relax the harm ceiling; `HOLD` or `FENCE` when evidence is thin
  *and* a hard ceiling is violated).

Pairwise reader agreement, same 67 segments: 1↔2 raw 0.7910 / kappa 0.6554,
1↔3 raw 0.9403 / kappa **0.8929**, 2↔3 raw 0.8507 / kappa 0.7498.

Do not quote any single rate as "accuracy": reader 1 and reader 3 agree tightly
with each other because they share a reading, and reader 2 dissents from the
engine only on the rule axis. Per-round detail: `rounds/roundN/README.md`.

### The second case set: round 4 and a model cross-check on the same 38 segments

The 30 new cases in `next_batch/` were built to reach inputs the first set never
tested. Two independent sheets answered them:

- a **human reader** at **0.8158** (31/38) — round 4, `rounds/round4/`
- a **language model** at **0.8947** (34/38) — `model_check/`, with
  `"kind": "model_check"`, **not** a round and not a fifth reader

The model's sheet is kept separate because a model reproducing the rule is a
different measurement from a person judging a release. Their answers show it: on
`nb-18` the model computed the 95% lower bound itself and fenced the segment,
while the reader used the 95% point estimate and released it.

**Where they agree:** both dissent on the same three cases — `nb-07`, `nb-08`,
`nb-11` — the G4 / fallback question below. **Where they part:** the reader also
dissents on `nb-18` (they took the 95% point estimate against a 90% floor; the
model computed the lower bound), on `nb-03` and `nb-04` (high and critical harm
with a human fallback behind them, open question 1), and on `nb-20` (a 1.0 floor
cannot be proven, so they answered `HOLD`). The model agrees with the engine on
all four. In the other direction the model alone dissents on `nb-01`, where it
wants a fallback from medium harm up. That is the point: the model follows the
rule, the reader asks whether anyone is standing behind the segment.

The model's four disagreements are one design choice seen twice:

- **G4's floor.** It fires from `high` up; the model wants a human fallback from
  `medium` up. Only `nb-01` can show that difference.
- **What counts as a fallback.** G4 fires only on `none`, so `degrade` and
  `rollback` count as contained damage; the model accepts neither. Related: G5
  loads `human_review_cost` only for `human`, so a 0.6 review cost on a
  degrade segment is priced at 0.02 against a 0.5 cap.

Simulated cost of adopting either reading: the G4 floor change flips 0 segments
in rounds 1–3 and 1 here; counting degrade/rollback as no fallback flips 1
(`ho-010/at_ceiling`) and 3 here. **Neither is implemented.** Adopting both would
take the model's sheet to 38/38 and this round to 34/38, and a rule tuned until
it matches whoever answered it has validated nothing. Recorded as open question 3
in `DESIGN.md`.

## Why this exists

`dev.jsonl` and `holdout.jsonl` were written by the author of the rules, with
one golden branch per gate. A perfect score against them proves only that the
engine follows its own rules. An independent annotator is the only way to
falsify the claim that those rules match a reasonable release judgement.

## What the annotator sees

`prompts.jsonl`, generated by `scripts/gen_blind_prompts.py`. Each row contains
only `id`, `suite` and `contract`. The fields `expected`, `why` and `kind` are
**deliberately removed** — `kind` would leak which gate is being exercised.
Do not hand-edit `prompts.jsonl`; regenerate it from the suites.

The annotator must NOT see:

- the engine's output for these cases,
- the scored sets or their golden labels,
- this repository's README claims about accuracy.

## The question to answer

For each segment in each contract, answer one question:

> Given this offline evidence and this contract, would you let the model act
> alone on this traffic? Answer `AUTO` (yes), `FENCE` (no, route to a human or
> disable), or `HOLD` (the evidence is not enough to decide).

Record why in your own words. Disagreements are the valuable output: a case
where a careful reader says `FENCE` and the engine says `AUTO` is a candidate
defect, not an annotation error to be smoothed away.

## Annotation file format

Put independent annotations at `eval/blind/annotations.jsonl`, one JSON object
per line:

```
{"id":"dev-001","verdicts":{"thin":"HOLD"}}
{"id":"ho-007","verdicts":{"five_hundred":"FENCE","one_thousand":"AUTO"}}
```

This file is gitignored on purpose: it is a measurement input, not a source
file, and it must not be silently committed as if it were a result.

## Scoring

```
python scripts/blind_eval.py
python scripts/blind_eval.py eval/blind/annotations.jsonl
python scripts/blind_eval.py eval/blind/annotations.jsonl --json
```

The script compares the annotator **against the engine's verdicts**, not
against the author-written golden labels. It reports agreement, a Wilson 95%
interval when the sample permits, and a full disagreement list. With no
annotation file it prints `NOT RUN` and invents no number.

Agreement here is not a model accuracy figure. It is a measure of how often an
independent reader agrees with deterministic gates, and its interval will be
wide until enough cases are annotated.

## Human-readable annotation packet

**One-file handout:** `盲评填写表.csv`, generated by
`python scripts/gen_single_sheet.py`. It contains every input field plus empty
`你的判定` and `理由` columns, with Chinese headers and a BOM for Excel / WPS.
Score a filled copy directly with
`python scripts/blind_eval.py <path-to-filled.csv>`.

Run `python scripts/gen_blind_packet.py` to regenerate the detailed packet
from the prompts:

- `ANNOTATOR_GUIDE.md` — self-contained instructions; contains no results,
  no answers and no accuracy figures.
- `worksheet.md` — one table per case with empty verdict and reason columns.
- `answer_sheet.template.csv` — one row per segment, empty verdict column.

Copy the template to `answer_sheet.csv` (gitignored), fill it in, then score it
with `python scripts/blind_eval.py eval/blind/answer_sheet.csv`. The script
accepts CSV or JSONL, skips undecided rows and reports how many segments remain
unannotated. Undecided rows are never guessed and never counted as agreement.

Project self-checks assert that the packet stays in sync with the prompts,
that every verdict cell stays empty in the shipped template, and that no
accuracy figure leaks into any file an annotator sees.

| 2 (2026-11-09) | a different reader, declared no access to round 1 or this repo | `rounds/round2/answers.csv`, every segment decided | yes — `rounds/round2/` | 0.9403; four disagreements, all rule precedence |
| 3 (2026-11-09) | a third reader, declared no access to any published round | `rounds/round3/answers.csv`, every segment decided | yes — `rounds/round3/` | 0.7910; ten G2 plus the same four rule cases |
| 4 (2026-11-09) | a fourth reader, declared no access to `rounds/` or `model_check/` | `rounds/round4/answers.csv`, **second case set**, every segment decided | yes — `rounds/round4/` | 0.8158 on 38 new segments; 7 = 2 Wilson + 5 rule |

Outside the series: `model_check/` holds a **model's** answers to the same second
case set at 0.8947. It is not a round and produces no kappa with any reader.
Round 4 is a human round but sits on a different case set, so it produces no kappa
with rounds 1–3 either.

The scoring script labels each disagreement with its cause
(`wilson_lower_below_floor_but_point_estimate_above` versus
`other_gate_or_judgement`), so rounds are comparable without hand analysis.

**Round 1's finding is a known conservative bias, not a defect to be patched
away**: gate G2 exists precisely to refuse point-estimate reasoning. A team that
deliberately wants point-estimate semantics should not use this tool.

**Round 2 changed what the disagreement is about.** That reader applied the same
Wilson lower bound independently (12 of their rationales quote it, all matching
`wilson_lower` within 0.05pp), so G2 stopped being contested. What remains is:

1. Does an existing human fallback relax the harm ceiling? This engine says no.
2. When evidence is thin *and* a hard ceiling is violated, is the answer `HOLD`
   or `FENCE`? G1 answers first, so the engine says `HOLD`; the reader said
   `FENCE`, which is arguably more useful because more data will not fix an
   absolute ceiling violation.

**Round 3 made both questions 2-of-3.** This reader used the point estimate (so
it reproduced round 1's ten G2 disagreements) *and* took the same side as round 2
on both rule questions, giving 14 = 10 + 4 and a clean decomposition with no
residual. Readers 1 and 3 differ on exactly the four rule cases; kappa 0.8929.

Both questions remain open in `DESIGN.md`, and the split is now explicit: the
engine is on the minority side of each. They are still not being patched on the
strength of two readers — changing verdict precedence invalidates every published
round — but the decision now has a measured cost (`scripts/whatif_rules.py`)
instead of being a matter of taste.

## Publishing a round

A round is publishable only if its sheet ships with it — a rate whose input
nobody can re-run is the unfalsifiable claim this project rejects. Use:

```
python scripts/publish_round.py --round N \
  --sheet eval/blind/rounds/roundN/answers.csv \
  --out   eval/blind/rounds/roundN/result.json \
  --date YYYY-MM-DD --annotator "<who and what they did not see>"
```

The script refuses to overwrite a different published result without `--force`,
and the self-check recomputes every published round from its sheet.

⚠️ **Any further round needs fresh annotators who have not read `rounds/`.** Once
answers are public, anyone who has seen them cannot produce a blind measurement.
`next_round/` holds a blank sheet and the admission rules; the project self-check
keeps that sheet blank and in sync. Treat two readers as a starting point, not a
distribution: kappa from n=2 has no interval.

`next_batch/` is the packet for the round after that. It carries **new cases, not
new readers on the same cases**: three readers exhausted the first 67 segments,
and a fourth reader on them buys one kappa point rather than a new rule
question. It has its own `prompts.jsonl` and its own blank `sheet.csv` with the
same columns. It has been answered by a model (`model_check/`, 0.8947) and by a
human reader (round 4, `rounds/round4/`, 0.8158); the model's sheet is not a
round and the two are never averaged. A second human reader can still be added on
these cases; score a returned
sheet with `blind_eval.py --prompts eval/blind/next_batch/prompts.jsonl`; without
that flag the script reads the first batch and rejects every row, which is
deliberate. Its cases were **selected for coverage, not sampled**, so its rate
must never be subtracted from the earlier ones or presented as a trend.
